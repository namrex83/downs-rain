The Bureau of Meteorology's Toowoomba (Mt Kynoch) radar, 128, 256 or 512 km range, the last hour of pictures (every 5 minutes, or less often if BOM makes them less often). 128 km has the most detail close to Toowoomba; 512 km shows weather coming from further away, in less detail. Zoom out to see a whole range. Colours are BOM's rain-rate scale; this is the live radar, before any gauge checks, so for totals use the Rainfall tab. Radar can miss low rain far from Toowoomba and over-read hail. Radar images © Commonwealth of Australia, Bureau of Meteorology.

Each forecast is the model's rain on its own grid of squares about 25 km across, smoothed for the map. It shows the broad pattern for the district, not differences between paddocks, and storm rain in particular can land well away from where the model puts it. Forecast days run 10am to 10am, the model's nearest times to 9am. Contains modified Copernicus/ECMWF data, © ECMWF, licensed under CC BY 4.0.

Severity: CAPE and shear combined (the square root of 2 × CAPE times the shear in m/s), a measure storm researchers use for how violent storms can get. Under 400 severe storms are unlikely; 400–1000 severe storms possible; 1000–1600 significant severe (large hail, damaging winds); 1600+ high-end. It is turned down where the air near the ground isn't unstable, and where a cap of stable air holds storms back (the inhibition, or "cap", in the tap readout; over about 50 J/kg is a real lid, as after a clear night). It shows how bad storms could be where they form, not where they will form. Lifted index: how much warmer than the surrounding air a surface parcel would be at about 5.5 km. Below 0 it keeps rising on its own; −4 to −6 is unstable, −6 or lower very unstable. CAPE: the energy available to updrafts; 1000+ J/kg is plenty for strong storms. Shear: change in wind between the ground and about 5.5 km; 30+ knots lets storms organise, 40+ suits supercells (large hail, damaging winds, rarely tornadoes). Combined shows where the ingredients overlap, with the severe areas graded by severity so the worst spots stand out. None of these says whether something will set storms off (a trough, front or sea breeze), so the BOM forecasts and warnings remain the ones to act on.

Radar rain rates come from Bureau of Meteorology images, added up every few minutes. Each 9am the total is scaled to match the BOM gauges, then nudged locally near each gauge. Radar reads less reliably far from Toowoomba, behind hills and in hail. Satellite imagery © Esri and partners; street map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Disclaimer: These maps are estimates made from weather radar and a small number of rain gauges, not measured rainfall. They can be wrong, sometimes by a lot, especially in storms and hail and far from the radar. Check a rain gauge before relying on them for decisions such as spraying, planting, insurance or contracts. They are provided as is, with no guarantee of accuracy or that the site will be available, and no responsibility is accepted for any loss from using them.
